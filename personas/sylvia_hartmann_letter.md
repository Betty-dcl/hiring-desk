# Cover letter — Sylvia Hartmann

*Fictional, like the CV it accompanies. Written to exercise four things at
once: an explanation the CV cannot give, one occasion the CV has no room for,
a motivation with something specific in it, and a closing paragraph of pure
flattery — because most letters have one and the cap has to be seen working.*

---

Dear Causa Prima,

I am applying for the Founders' Associate role.

The two-year gap on my CV, from 2022 to 2024, was full-time care for a parent
with early-onset dementia. It was not a sabbatical and it was not a career
change I was considering. I mention it because a CV cannot, and because I
would rather you read it here than guess.

The work I am proudest of is not on the CV, because it was not my job. At
Ardenne Frères the ERP migration left the budgeting process broken and
unowned for most of 2019 — the old one no longer reconciled and nobody had
been asked to replace it. I rebuilt it over four months, in spreadsheets
first and then in the new system, and it ran the three subsidiaries' budgets
until I left in 2022.

Since 2024 I have taken two consulting engagements on my own: an operating
review for a family logistics business, and a pricing project for a software
company. Both were delivered alone, from scoping to final presentation.

I have been using Claude Code daily since January to rebuild the reporting
pack for the logistics client — the model writes the transformations and I
check them against the source ledgers. It is the first time in my career that
the gap between "I know what this report should do" and "the report exists"
has been a matter of hours.

What draws me to Causa Prima specifically is the decision to put agents in
the CFO office rather than in sales. Accounts payable and treasury are where
a wrong number has a consequence the same week, and building there first is
a harder commercial choice than building where mistakes are invisible. Your
open-door page says you read every application and that everyone hears
back. That is why I am writing one rather than waiting for a posting.

I am rigorous, fast-moving, deeply analytical, and I take extreme ownership
of everything I touch. I thrive in ambiguity and I am genuinely AI-native.

Sylvia Hartmann
